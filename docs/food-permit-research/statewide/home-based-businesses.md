# Home-Based Food Businesses: CFO and MEHKO

## Cottage Food Operation (CFO)
CDPH describes CFOs as home-kitchen operations preparing and packaging certain non-potentially hazardous foods. Products must appear on the current CDPH Approved Cottage Food List.

- **Class A:** Local LEA registration; direct-to-consumer sales.
- **Class B:** Local LEA permit; direct sales and qualifying indirect sales through retail food facilities.

CDPH does not register or permit CFOs; the local LEA does. Application, inspection, fee, and local process depend on jurisdiction.

### Product screening
Do not infer eligibility from a food's common name. Capture exact product, ingredients, shelf stability, preparation/packaging method, and sales channel. Compare the product with the current CDPH approved list.

### CFO training
CDPH says anyone preparing or packaging cottage food must complete a food processor course within three months of registration or permitting and every three years. CDPH identifies ANSI-accredited food handler courses as satisfying this requirement.

### 2026 CFO sales caps — unresolved
CDPH's general CFO overview page surfaced with a Class A limit of $75,000, conflicting with the supplied Gemini report's adjusted 2026 figures ($88,878 Class A; $177,756 Class B). The earlier research audit identified a dated CDPH 2026 adjustment PDF, but its exact values and effective date must be re-opened and confirmed before production use.

**Do not hard-code either value until the dated adjustment notice is validated.**

## Microenterprise Home Kitchen Operation (MEHKO)
A MEHKO is a locally authorized home-based food facility. CDPH says a city or county must adopt an ordinance or resolution before a local health permit can be issued. Availability is jurisdiction-specific.

CDPH's general requirements page lists application to the LEA, facility inspection, approved food manager certification, direct sales between MEHKO and customer, and same-day preparation/cooking/service.

### Stale numeric guidance warning
The CDPH general MEHKO page surfaced with older figures: 30 meals/day, 60 meals/week, and $50,000 gross annual sales. The supplied Gemini report cites a 2026 cap of $110,442 and 30 meals/day, 90 meals/week. Do not hard-code either set until the current statute and dated CDPH adjustment notice are checked. Local rules may add restrictions.

### Internet Food Service Intermediaries (IFSI)
CDPH says an IFSI must register with CDPH before advertising or promoting MEHKOs on its website or mobile application. This is distinct from the MEHKO's local permit and from who physically delivers food. Do not collapse these into a blanket third-party-delivery statement.

### One home-based operation per residence
CDPH's CFO/MEHKO FAQ says a residence cannot operate both a CFO and MEHKO, cannot aggregate multiple operations to exceed statutory limits, and cannot have multiple residents each operating separate CFOs/MEHKOs from the same residence.

## Comparison
| Attribute | CFO | MEHKO |
|---|---|---|
| Main food type | Approved non-potentially hazardous cottage foods | Prepared meals subject to program rules |
| Local authorization | Class A registration or Class B permit | Local ordinance/resolution plus local health permit |
| Sales model | Class A direct; Class B direct and qualifying indirect | CDPH general page says direct sale between operator and customer |
| Training | Food processor course; ANSI handler course may satisfy | Approved food manager certification listed by CDPH |
| Numeric limits | 2026 adjusted limits need verification | 2026 sales and meal limits need verification |

If neither pathway fits, CDPH says the operation may need a local retail food permit or CDPH PFR, depending on the activity.
