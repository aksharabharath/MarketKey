# California Statewide Framework

## Governing law
The California Retail Food Code (CalCode) is part of California Health and Safety Code, Division 104, Part 7. CDPH's official code index organizes provisions covering definitions, management and personnel, general food safety, equipment and facilities, specialized operations, and compliance/enforcement.

CDPH states that CalCode contains structural, equipment, and operational requirements for California retail food facilities and is primarily enforced by 62 local environmental health regulatory agencies. CDPH provides technical support.

**MarketKey implication:** CalCode is the statewide baseline, but the responsible LEA must be identified for the user's address before showing local application steps, fees, or inspections.

## Local permits and plan review
Local agencies generally administer retail food facility permits and operational oversight. CalCode § 114380 addresses plan review for proposed construction or remodeling. The code text surfaced through CDPH says plans and specifications must be submitted for review and approval before new construction or remodeling; an LEA may also require plans for other changes, including menu or method-of-operation changes, when needed for compliance.

Do not promise a universal local timeline. The code includes a 20-working-day plan decision provision, but that is not an end-to-end permit-processing guarantee. Completeness review, corrections, building approvals, and inspections may affect timing.

## Roles
- **CDPH:** Publishes CalCode, statewide program guidance, approved CFO food lists, and certain state registrations.
- **LEA:** Typically registers or permits CFOs, issues local retail food permits, conducts inspections, and sets local administrative processes and fees.
- **Other agencies:** Building, fire, planning/zoning, public works, wastewater, and business licensing agencies may have separate approvals.

## Activities outside a standard retail permit
CDPH explains that operations beyond CFO or MEHKO scope may need a local retail food permit or, depending on the activity, a CDPH Processed Food Registration (PFR). PFR exemptions are activity-specific. Do not decide based only on a generic description like “packaged food”; capture processing, packaging, storage, and sales details.

## Jurisdiction exceptions
Some cities operate independent environmental health programs. The Bay Area pilot identifies Berkeley as a separate city jurisdiction. The statewide resolver should use an address/city-to-LEA mapping, not simply `county -> agency`.
