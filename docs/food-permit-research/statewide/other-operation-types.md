# Commercial, Mobile, and Temporary Food Operations

Exact local permit, facility, commissary, event, and fee requirements must be checked with the responsible LEA.

## Permanent retail food facility
Restaurants, markets, and other retail food facilities are subject to CalCode structural, equipment, and operational requirements. New construction and remodeling can trigger plan review under HSC § 114380.

Ask about new facility/remodel/ownership change, menu and processes, layout/equipment, and fee-tier inputs only when required by the current local schedule. Other approvals (building, fire, planning, wastewater) may also apply.

## Mobile Food Facilities (MFF) and Compact Mobile Food Operations (CMFO)
A truck/trailer and a compact cart/stand are distinct categories. Ask about:
- Vehicle/cart type and dimensions.
- Whether food is prepared on the unit or elsewhere.
- Food types and processes.
- Open food versus specified prepackaged products/whole produce.
- Commissary or approved support-facility arrangements.
- Operating locations (private property, public right-of-way, events, multiple jurisdictions).

Do not apply an exemption from a short description alone. Check exact statutory criteria and local agency interpretation.

## Temporary Food Facilities (TFF)
TFFs generally relate to food booths at qualifying community events. Ask about event/organizer, dates and operating days, booth setup, menu, preparation location, and whether the vendor is part of an existing permitted operation.

The supplied report cites a 25-operating-days-in-90-days limit. Validate the exact code section and scope before using this as a rule.

## Catering and host-facility service
Catering, pop-ups, and temporary event booths are not synonymous. Ask whether food is prepared for a specific client/event, where service occurs, which permitted kitchen prepares/stores the food, and whether a truck/cart or temporary booth is used. Do not assume the host's permit covers the vendor.

## Processed Food Registration (PFR)
CDPH maintains a separate PFR program and lists activity-specific exemptions, including certain retail food facilities already holding a valid local permit. This is not a general exemption for all packaged products. Capture processing and distribution details and refer borderline cases to CDPH.
